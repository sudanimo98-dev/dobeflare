FAQ ▸
What is this?
DobeFlare is a demo tool that simulates how a Trust & Safety team triages incoming incidents by severity. Instead of relying on gut feel or whoever escalates the loudest, it scores each incident using a structured model and outputs a priority tier with an SLA target.
How do I use it?
Fill in the incident form on the left — pick a threat category, set blast radius, severity, and velocity sliders, and check any escalation flags that apply. Hit Submit and it scores and queues the incident automatically. Or click "Load examples" to see pre-built scenarios across all four priority tiers.
How does the scoring work?
Each incident is scored out of 100 across five factors: threat category base weight (up to 30 pts), blast radius — how many users or systems are affected (up to 25 pts), severity (up to 20 pts), velocity — how fast it's spreading (up to 15 pts), and escalation flags like press exposure, legal involvement, or executive visibility (up to 12 pts). The total determines the priority tier: P0 ≥ 70, P1 ≥ 45, P2 ≥ 22, P3 below that.
Why those category weights?
The weights reflect real-world impact and blast radius potential. Malware (30) and Legal (28) sit highest because they spread fast and carry regulatory or infrastructure consequences. AI & Synthetic (25) and Fraud (22) follow. Content Policy violations (12) sit lowest — still important, but typically contained to specific content with slower spread. The weights aren't arbitrary; they're calibrated to match the urgency a T&S team would assign in practice.
Is this a real production tool?
No — it's a portfolio demo. Built to show how I think about incident prioritization structure, not as production software. The scoring model is based on real triage frameworks I worked with across Google and TikTok, adapted into a standalone tool.
Why did you build it?
While interviewing for incident response roles, I kept getting asked how I prioritize when everything looks equally urgent. It reminded me of a real problem at Google — multiple P1s landing simultaneously with no structured way to differentiate them beyond gut feel. DobeFlare is the tool I wish we'd had.
